Analog design

CMOS Temperature Sensor & Reliability-Aware Analog Design

CTAT/PTAT sensing core with a Miller-compensated two-stage op-amp, characterised over PVT, mismatch, and aging

Cadence Virtuoso · Spectre · Maestro · GPDK45

Role in the project

Sole author: sensing architecture, transistor-level design of the sensing core and the two-stage op-amp, device sizing and bias-current selection, compensation design, temperature and PVT characterisation, Monte Carlo mismatch analysis, reliability aging analysis, and full-custom layout with post-layout evaluation.

Results

Temperature range
−25 to 125°C
Span
150°C
CTAT bias
2 µA
Sensitivity
−0.201 mV/°C

The 2 µA bias point was selected because it holds monotonic operation across the full 150°C span — monotonicity was the selection criterion, not raw sensitivity.

Architecture

A CTAT/PTAT sensing core produces the temperature-dependent signal, read out through a Miller-compensated two-stage operational amplifier.

CTAT / PTAT core→Bias selection→Two-stage op-amp→Compensation→Readout

Analog design considerations

  • Biasing — bias current was swept and evaluated for sensitivity, monotonicity, and power, with 2 µA chosen as the operating point.
  • Op-amp design — a two-stage topology for gain, with Miller compensation setting the dominant pole and therefore the phase margin.
  • Device sizing — tuned jointly against sensitivity, gain, stability, and power rather than for any single figure.
  • Temperature behaviour — VTH, gm, and ID trends were extracted across the sweep to understand where the sensing slope comes from and where it degrades.
  • PVT analysis — corner simulation to confirm behaviour holds outside the nominal case.
  • Monte Carlo mismatch — statistical spread of the sensing characteristic under device mismatch.
  • Reliability aging — MOSRA-based analysis of how device degradation shifts the sensor characteristic over operating lifetime.
  • Layout considerations — matching-driven placement for the sensing devices, since mismatch appears directly as a temperature-reading offset.

Characterisation

Key learnings

  • Monotonicity across the full range is worth more than a steeper slope over part of it.
  • Mismatch, not sensitivity, sets the practical accuracy floor of a sensor like this.
  • Aging analysis changes which bias point looks correct — the best day-one point is not always the best end-of-life point.
